## 1. Maricopa County heat-associated deaths — final annual reports (2023, 2024, 2025)

Source reports (Maricopa County Dept. of Public Health, Division of Epidemiology & Informatics):
- **2023 report** ("2023 Heat Related Deaths Report," last updated April 2024) — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820
- **2024 report** ("2024 Heat-Related Deaths Report," May 2025) — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934
- **2025 report** ("2025 Heat-Related Deaths Report," April 2026) — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510

### 2023
- Total confirmed heat-related deaths: 645 — "Maricopa County identified a total of 645 heat related deaths occurring in 2023." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820 (p.7)
- Share experiencing homelessness: 45% — "Forty-five percent of heat related deaths were among people experiencing homelessness." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820 (p.4 infographic); confirmed again on p.15: "Individuals experiencing homelessness make up the largest proportion of heat related deaths in 2023" with bar chart value "45% Homeless"
- Share outdoors: 75% — "In 2023, 75% of heat related deaths occurred outdoors." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820 (p.13)
- Share indoors: 25% (complement of the above, per same outdoor/indoor bar chart, p.13)
- Share involving substances: 65% — "Sixty-five percent of heat related deaths involved substances in 2023." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820 (p.4 infographic)
- Cooling-center awareness/use of decedents: not addressed in this report. It does note that MCDPH "conducted an evaluation of cooling centers during the summer of 2023" (i.e., the CDC MMWR study covered in Section 2) — "In an effort to identify barriers, awareness, and utilization of heat relief centers in Maricopa County, MCDPH also conducted an evaluation of cooling centers during the summer of 2023." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820 (p.6)
- Additional context: 80% of heat-related deaths were among Maricopa County residents; two-thirds were age 50+; 59% were heat-caused (vs. heat-contributed). — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5820 (p.4)

### 2024
- Total confirmed heat-related deaths: 608 — "As of 3/31/2025, Maricopa County identified a total of 608 heat-related deaths occurring in 2024. This shows the first year-over-year decrease in heat-related deaths observed in Maricopa County since 2014." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934 (p.6)
- Share experiencing homelessness: 49% — "People experiencing homelessness (PEH) in Maricopa County made up the largest proportion (49%) of heat-related deaths in 2024 compared to any other living situation." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934 (p.14)
- Share outdoors: 77% — "Heat-related deaths in outdoor settings continue to drive the total number of deaths. In 2024, 77% of heat-related deaths occurred outdoors." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934 (p.12)
- Share indoors: 23% (same chart, p.12)
- Share involving substances: 57% — "In 2024, Maricopa County identified 349 deaths involving substance use, accounting for 57% of all heat-related deaths." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934 (p.15)
- Of substance-involved deaths, stimulants (e.g., methamphetamine): 89% — "Among heat-related deaths involving substances, nearly all (89%) involved stimulants in 2024." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934 (p.15)
- Cooling-center awareness/use of decedents: not addressed in this report.
- Additional context: 78% male; ~60% aged 50+; 76.3% Maricopa County residents; 45% had cardiovascular disease comorbidity. — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/5934 (pp.4, 9)

### 2025
- Total confirmed heat-related deaths: 430 — "During 2025, Maricopa County identified 430 heat-related deaths." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.5)
- Share experiencing homelessness: 49% (overview infographic) / 48% (living-situation chart) — "49% of heat-related deaths were among people experiencing homelessness." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.5); the dedicated "Living Situation" chart states "People experiencing homelessness (PEH) in Maricopa County made up the largest proportion (49%) of heat-related deaths in 2025 compared with any other living situation" with a bar labeled "Experiencing Homelessness 48%" — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.15) [note: report text says 49%, the chart bar itself is labeled 48% — both are quoted verbatim from the same report]
- Share outdoors: 74% — "In 2025, 74% of heat-related deaths occurred outdoors." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.13)
- Share indoors: 27% (same chart, p.13) — chart shows "73%" outdoor / "27%" indoor for 2025 in the multi-year bar and "74%" in the callout text; both figures are from the same page.
- Share involving substances: 55% — "55% of heat-related deaths involved substances in 2025." — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.5)
- Cooling-center awareness/use of decedents: not addressed in this report.
- Additional context: 84% among Maricopa County residents; 74% male; ~60% aged 50+; 47% had cardiovascular disease comorbidity; second consecutive year-over-year decline. — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.5)

### Trend summary across all three PDF reports (bar-chart data, consistent across all three documents)
- Total heat-related deaths by year: 2021: 339; 2022: 425; 2023: 645; 2024: 608; 2025: 430 — https://www.maricopa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/6510 (p.7 chart)

None of the three MCDPH annual reports directly states whether individual decedents used or were aware of cooling centers — that specific awareness/use/barriers question is answered by the CDC MMWR field assessment (Section 2 below), which the 2023 MCDPH report itself references as the source of that evaluation.

## 2. CDC MMWR "Notes from the Field" — Cooling Center Awareness, Use, and Access Barriers, Maricopa County, Aug 1–Sept 15, 2023

Source: MMWR Vol. 74, No. 14 (April 24, 2025), "Notes from the Field: Assessment of Awareness, Use, and Access Barriers to Cooling Centers in Maricopa County, Arizona — August 1–September 15, 2023" — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm

- Background trend: "During 2014–2023, the number of heat-related deaths in Maricopa County, Arizona, increased from 61 to 645, and the number of cooling centers doubled to 112." — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Awareness of cooling centers: "Many cooling center visitors (68%) and public respondents (61%) were aware of cooling centers before the survey" — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Frequency of use: among those who had visited cooling centers in the previous 30 days, "49% of visitors and 41% of the public visited five or more times" — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — lack of awareness (didn't know where they were / didn't know cooling centers existed): 36% of visitors and 49% of the public cited this — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — location uncertainty (didn't know exact location): 17% of visitors and 22% of the public — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — lack of transportation: 31% of visitors cited this as a barrier — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — operating hours ("not open when I can access them"): 14% of visitors — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — pets not allowed / uncertainty about pet policy: 7% of visitors, 12% of the public — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — overcrowding ("too crowded"): 14% of visitors — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — safety concerns: 11% of both visitors and the public — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Barrier — nowhere to store belongings: 10% of visitors — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm
- Interventions implemented as a result: "To increase awareness and visibility of and access to cooling centers, MCDPH incorporated the following into its community heat action plan: expanding operation hours until 7 p.m. or later at 17 centers, making additional street signage available, and funding a heat-relief call center staffed by bilingual health workers to facilitate location of and transportation to and from cooling centers." — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm

## 3. Overture Maps places schema (as of 2026) — opening hours field and cooling-center/heat-relief category

### Opening hours
- The `place` entity schema (Places theme) does **not** include an opening-hours property. Confirmed by direct review of the schema reference page: the property list for `place` is: `id`, `bbox`, `geometry`, `theme`, `type`, `version`, `sources[]`, `operating_status`, `basic_category`, `taxonomy`, `confidence`, `websites`, `socials`, `emails`, `phones`, `brand`, `addresses[]`, `names` — https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/places/place/
- The schema's only closure-adjacent field, `operating_status`, is explicitly scoped to permanent open/closed status, not hours: "This is not an indication of opening hours or that the place is open/closed at the current time-of-day or day-of-week." — https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/places/place/
- An `OpeningHours` property does exist in the Overture schema, but it belongs to the **Transportation** theme only (used by `AccessRule.When`, `ProhibitedTransitionRule.When`, and `SpeedLimitRule.When` for road/segment access rules), not to Places: "Time span or time spans during which something is open or active, specified in the OpenStreetMap opening hours specification" — https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/common/scoping/opening_hours/ (type: `string`; scoped to transportation rule objects, not the `place` entity)

### Cooling-center / heat-relief category
- Overture's places taxonomy (~2,118 category rows in the `overture_categories.csv` reference list, retrieved from a pinned commit since the `categories` property itself was removed from the schema on 2026-09-23) contains **no category for "cooling," "heat relief," "warming center," or "resilience hub."** Direct grep of the full CSV confirms zero matches for "cooling," "heat," "warming," "resilience," "respite," and only an unrelated "iv_hydration" (a medical clinic category) match for "hydration." — https://github.com/OvertureMaps/schema/blob/96ec26830d5b1c83216de3ce1a91c301c178705e/docs/schema/concepts/by-theme/places/overture_categories.csv
- The only "shelter" categories present are `animal_shelter; [pets,animal_shelter]` and `homeless_shelter; [public_service_and_government,organization,social_service_organizations,homeless_shelter]` — neither of which is a seasonal/emergency cooling or heat-relief designation. — https://github.com/OvertureMaps/schema/blob/96ec26830d5b1c83216de3ce1a91c301c178705e/docs/schema/concepts/by-theme/places/overture_categories.csv
- As of the 2026-09-23 schema release (one month after the 2026-08-19.0 data release referenced in this analysis), Overture formally removed the old `categories` property from the places schema entirely, replacing it with `taxonomy`/`basic_category`: "removed the `categories` property entirely, including `categories.primary` and `categories.alternate`. Use `taxonomy` (`taxonomy.primary`, `taxonomy.hierarchy`, `taxonomy.alternates`) or `basic_category` instead." — https://docs.overturemaps.org/blog/2026/09/23/release-notes/
- The `taxonomy` property's own definition confirms it is a categorical classification field with no climate/emergency-shelter-specific extension: "A structured representation of the place's category within the Overture taxonomy. Provides the primary classification, full hierarchy path, and alternate categories." — https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/places/place/

**Conclusion for Section 3**: Overture Maps places data, at both the 2026-08-19.0 release and the schema version current as of the September 2026 release notes, has (a) no dedicated opening-hours field on the `place` entity, and (b) no taxonomy category for cooling centers, heat-relief sites, or warming/cooling shelters — confirmed directly against the category CSV and schema reference docs rather than inferred.

- What it is / who runs it: the network is a joint effort of the Maricopa Association of Governments (MAG) and the Maricopa County Department of Public Health (MCDPH), in partnership with municipalities and nonprofits: "The Maricopa County Department of Public Health (MCDPH) and the Maricopa Association of Governments (MAG) officially launched the 2026 Heat Relief Network (HRN) on May 1." — https://www.maricopa.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/3652
- The HRN is described as: "a voluntary partnership comprised of service providers, faith‐based groups, municipalities, businesses, and nonprofit agencies that mobilize to provide free water and heat refuge" — reported via search-indexed MAG program description, cross-confirmed by https://www.maricopa.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/3652 [UNVERIFIED as an exact primary-source quote: azmag.gov's own HRN pages (azmag.gov/Programs/Heat-Relief-Network, hrn.azmag.gov, azmag.gov Portal PDFs) are behind a Cloudflare bot-challenge and could not be directly fetched during this research session, despite repeated attempts with different user agents; this description is drawn from a search-engine synthesis of azmag.gov content, not a page this session could independently render and quote in full]

- Season dates: "The HRN provides free, cooled spaces and other resources to the public through September 30" and launches "May 1" — https://www.maricopa.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/3652; confirmed independently: "The map goes live each year on May 1, with outreach partners who offer water and other services through September 30." — https://www.abc15.com/news/local-news/region-phoenix-metro/where-to-find-heat-relief-in-the-valley-in-2026-cooling-centers-hydration-stations-and-more

## 5. Novelty check — existing studies comparing official cooling-center/heat-relief lists to OSM/Overture/Google Maps/other map apps

**No study or article was found that directly compares an official cooling-center or heat-relief-network list against OpenStreetMap, Overture Maps, Google Maps, or another consumer map application's coverage.** This conclusion is based on multiple targeted searches (queries combining "cooling center"/"heat relief" with "OpenStreetMap," "Overture Maps," "Google Maps," "missing," "coverage," "bias," "audit," and "tagging") conducted on 2026-09-27. Closest related work found, none of which performs this comparison:

- Erin O'Neil & Sarah Tymochko, "Evaluating Cooling Center Coverage Using Persistent Homology of a Filtered Witness Complex" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09067 (also https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09067). This paper evaluates spatial gaps in cooling-center coverage in four US cities (central Boston, MA; central Austin, TX; Portland, OR; and Miami, FL) using a topological-data-analysis method, and compares its results to a Heat Vulnerability Index (HVI): "PH and the HVI identify different locations as vulnerable, thus indicating a potential value of assessing vulnerability from multiple perspectives." Its abstract does not mention OpenStreetMap, Overture Maps, or Google Maps as a comparison point or as the source of its cooling-center location data — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09067
- General OSM/Overture data-quality literature exists (e.g., HeiGIT's "OSM Completeness with Overture maps data," https://heigit.org/osm-completeness-with-overture-maps-data/; "A Global Comparison of Restaurants in OpenStreetMap and Overture Places," https://surprisedatespot.com/blog/comparing-overture-osm-restaurants/) but these compare general POI categories (e.g., restaurants) between OSM and Overture, not cooling centers/heat-relief sites specifically, and were not examined against any official government list.
- No papers or articles were found applying this comparison methodology to Maricopa County, MAG's Heat Relief Network, or any other U.S. heat-relief program specifically.

**Conclusion for Section 5**: based on the searches performed, this appears to be a novel comparison — pairing an official, government-run heat-relief-site list against Overture Maps place coverage has not been previously published.
